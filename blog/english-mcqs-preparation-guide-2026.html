English MCQs for PPSC & FPSC 2026: Grammar, Vocabulary & Smart Practice

📅 October 13, 2026✍️ MCQs Tayyari Team⏱️ 8 min read

Ask toppers what separated them from the rest and a surprising number will say the same thing: English. It appears in nearly every competitive paper in Pakistan, its questions repeat in predictable patterns, and unlike Current Affairs it never goes stale. Master it once, and it pays you back in every exam you ever attempt.

This guide covers the three battlegrounds of English MCQs — grammar, vocabulary, and comprehension — with the topics that actually repeat and a practice method that actually works.

Battleground 1: Grammar — the highest-return topics

You don't need a linguistics degree. A small set of grammar topics produces the majority of English MCQs in PPSC, FPSC, NTS and similar papers. Master these first:

  • Tenses — the single biggest source. Present/past perfect, continuous forms, and tense consistency in sentences.
  • Prepositions — pure memory, endlessly repeated (interested in, capable of, accused of). Learn them in fixed phrases, not isolation.
  • Articles — a/an/the rules, especially with countable vs uncountable nouns and superlatives.
  • Active & passive voice — conversion rules for every tense; a guaranteed 2–3 questions in most papers.
  • Direct & indirect narration — reported speech rules, tense backshifting, pronoun changes.
  • Subject–verb agreement — singular/plural traps, "neither/nor", collective nouns.
  • Conditionals & modals — if-clauses (types 1–3), would/should/must/might usage.

The method: learn one rule family per day, then immediately solve 50+ MCQs on just that rule. Rules without same-day MCQ practice evaporate within a week.

Battleground 2: Vocabulary — build it the MCQ way

Most aspirants "read" vocabulary. Toppers test it. Vocabulary MCQs come in four predictable forms:

  • Synonyms & antonyms — the bulk of vocabulary marks. Build a personal list of 500 high-frequency words (previous papers are the best source for which words).
  • Idioms & phrases — learn 10 new idioms daily with an example sentence each; revise weekly or they fade.
  • One-word substitution — "a person who speaks many languages" → polyglot. These are pure memory and very scoring.
  • Phrasal verbs — look after, put off, run into. Learn them as verb + particle pairs with meanings.

The daily habit that works: 20 new words + 80 revision MCQs, every single day. New words without revision are just entertainment. Keep a small notebook (or phone note) where every word gets an example sentence — words learned in sentences stick; words learned in lists don't.

Battleground 3: Comprehension & error detection

Error detection / sentence correction

These questions give you a sentence and ask where the mistake is. The technique: read the sentence once for meaning, then scan it part by part — check the verb first (tense/agreement), then prepositions, then articles. Most errors hide in exactly these three places. If a sentence "sounds fine," check the preposition — that's where examiners love to plant traps.

Reading comprehension

Don't read the passage first. Read the questions first, then scan the passage for answers. This cuts your time per passage dramatically and keeps you focused on what's actually asked. Answer in your own words first, then match to the closest option — this defeats the "almost right" distractor options.

5 mistakes that keep English scores low

  1. Memorizing rules without solving MCQs. English is tested as MCQs — your preparation must be MCQ-shaped.
  2. Cramming vocabulary lists without revision. 500 words revised five times beat 2,000 words seen once.
  3. Ignoring prepositions and articles as "too small to matter" — they're among the most-tested topics precisely because everyone ignores them.
  4. Translating from Urdu in your head. English prepositions and tenses don't map one-to-one from Urdu; learn the English patterns directly.
  5. Practicing without a timer. In the real paper you get under a minute per question — train at that pace from day one.

Your 4-week English plan

  1. Week 1 — Grammar core: tenses, prepositions, articles. One rule family per day + 50 MCQs on it the same day.
  2. Week 2 — Grammar advanced: voice, narration, subject–verb agreement, conditionals. Start the daily 20-words habit.
  3. Week 3 — Vocabulary assault: synonyms/antonyms, idioms, one-word substitution. 100+ mixed English MCQs daily.
  4. Week 4 — Exam simulation: full English sections under timed conditions, error-detection drills, and revision of your personal word list only.

Key takeaways
  • Grammar: master tenses, prepositions, articles, voice, narration, agreement, conditionals — they repeat endlessly.
  • Vocabulary: 20 new words daily + 80 revision MCQs; learn words in sentences, not lists.
  • Error detection: check verb → preposition → article, in that order.
  • Comprehension: read questions first, then scan the passage.
  • Practice in MCQ format under timed conditions — English rewards volume.

Frequently Asked Questions

How important is English in PPSC and FPSC exams?

Very. English appears as a dedicated section in FPSC screening tests and throughout the General Ability portion of PPSC one-paper exams — it's one of the most predictable scoring areas.

How can I improve English vocabulary quickly?

There's no shortcut, but the fastest honest method is 20 new words daily with example sentences, plus 80 revision MCQs on old words. Previous papers show you exactly which words repeat.

Which grammar topics should I prepare first?

Tenses, prepositions, and articles — in that order. They produce the most MCQs and the rules are finite and learnable.
