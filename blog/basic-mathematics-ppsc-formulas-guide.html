Basic Mathematics for PPSC: Formulas That Carry 10+ Marks

📅 October 19, 2026✍️ MCQs Tayyari Team⏱️ 9 min read

Ask ten PPSC aspirants which section they skip first, and eight will say the same word: math. It's the section most candidates leave to the end — and the section where a two-week effort buys the cheapest marks in the entire paper. In a typical PPSC one-paper test, basic mathematics quietly carries 8–12 marks: percentages, ratios, averages, a little algebra, profit and loss. You learned them all before matric — you just need to wake them up.

This guide is not a math textbook. It's the short list of formulas that show up in PPSC and FPSC MCQ papers, how examiners disguise them, and a 14-day plan that turns math from your weakest section into a scoring machine.

Why Basic Math Is the Easiest 10 Marks in PPSC

Think about the trade-off. An Islamiat fact or a Pak Studies date has to be memorised one by one, and the paper might not even ask them. But one percentage formula — percentage = (part ÷ whole) × 100 — answers four or five different questions on the same paper. Math marks are multiplied: learn one tool, apply it again and again. No other General Ability subject gives you that return.

There's a second, quieter advantage: because so many aspirants skip math, the questions stay predictable. PPSC examiners recycle the same five or six clusters year after year. Master the clusters, and you walk into the hall having already solved half the math section before.

The Formula List That Actually Matters

Forget everything you were taught in FSc. PPSC basic mathematics lives in six clusters. Here they are, in the order of their weight:

1. Percentages — the king cluster

Nearly every math section opens with a percentage question. Lock in these:

  • Percentage = (Part ÷ Whole) × 100 — e.g., 15% of 240 = (15 × 240) ÷ 100 = 36.
  • Percentage increase/decrease = (Difference ÷ Original) × 100 — a salary rising from Rs. 40,000 to Rs. 46,000 is a 15% increase.
  • Reverse percentage: if 20% of a number is 60, the number is 60 ÷ 0.20 = 300.

2. Ratio, proportion and partnership

  • If a : b = 2 : 3 and the total is 50, divide 50 by (2+3) = 5 → 20 and 30.
  • Direct proportion: if 5 workers finish a job in 12 days, 10 workers take 6 days (inverse proportion flips the ratio).
  • Average speed on equal distances: 2ab ÷ (a + b).

3. Averages

  • Average = Sum of values ÷ Number of values.
  • Sum of first n natural numbers = n(n+1) ÷ 2 — the sum of 1 to 100 is 5050, a question PPSC has asked in multiple forms.
  • When a new value joins a group, add it to the total and divide again — don't recompute from scratch.

4. Basic algebra — just three identities

You don't need the whole of algebra. Three identities answer nearly every PPSC algebra MCQ:

  • (a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b²
  • (a − b)² = a² − 2ab + b²
  • a² − b² = (a + b)(a − b)

Plus the golden shortcut: if x + 1/x = a, then x² + 1/x² = a² − 2. Examiners love it because it looks hard.

5. Profit, loss and interest

  • Profit % = (Profit ÷ Cost Price) × 100 — remember: always divide by the cost price, never the selling price.
  • Simple Interest = (Principal × Rate × Time) ÷ 100.
  • Discount: a 20% discount on Rs. 500 means you pay 80% — Rs. 400.

6. Speed, time, distance and age problems

  • Distance = Speed × Time. To convert km/h to m/s, multiply by 5/18; m/s to km/h, multiply by 18/5.
  • Age problems: "father is 3 times his son's age; sum is 48" → son = 48 ÷ 4 = 12. The trick is always to set up one equation with one unknown.
  • Number series: look for differences, squares (1, 4, 9, 16…), cubes, and ×2±1 patterns.

How Examiners Disguise the Same Formulas

Here's the part coaching centres charge for: PPSC math MCQs are rarely about the formula itself — they're about recognising it under a story. "A shopkeeper marks an item 25% above cost and gives a 10% discount" is just two percentage operations chained together. "The ratio of boys to girls is 3:2 and there are 15 girls" is the same division you practised above.

This is why formula lists alone don't convert to marks. You need to see each formula dressed up in five different stories, which is exactly what MCQ practice does. Your 14-Day Math Plan

  1. Days 1–2: Percentages. Memorise the three formulas, solve 60–80 MCQs. This is your highest-weight cluster — don't rush it.
  2. Days 3–4: Ratio, proportion and averages. The n(n+1)/2 formula and ratio-splitting method until they're reflex.
  3. Days 5–6: Algebra identities. Just the three identities plus the x + 1/x shortcut. Drill 40–50 MCQs.
  4. Days 7–8: Profit/loss, interest, discount. One session of formulas, one session of mixed practice.
  5. Days 9–10: Speed/distance, age problems, number series. These are story-heavy — practice reading them fast.
  6. Days 11–12: Mixed timed sets. 50 MCQs in 45 minutes. No calculator — the exam hall won't have one either.
  7. Days 13–14: Error review. Redo every question you got wrong. If the same formula keeps tripping you, write it on a flashcard and revise it before bed.

3 Mistakes That Eat Your Math Marks

  1. Solving with a calculator in practice. Mental arithmetic is part of the skill. If you always reach for the calculator, the exam's time pressure will crush you.
  2. Reading the question once. Most wrong answers in math aren't wrong formulas — they're misread numbers. Read every word problem twice; the first read finds the formula, the second finds the actual numbers.
  3. Learning formulas without options. MCQ math is half elimination: if a discount question gives options above the original price, you can reject them without solving. Practise with the four options, not just the formula.
Key takeaways
  • Basic math carries 8–12 predictable marks in the PPSC one paper — the highest return per hour of any General Ability section.
  • Six clusters cover nearly everything: percentages, ratio/proportion, averages, three algebra identities, profit/loss/interest, speed–distance–age.
  • Examiners disguise the same formulas in stories — only MCQ practice teaches you to recognise them fast.
  • Practise without a calculator, read every question twice, and eliminate options before solving.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many math questions come in the PPSC one paper exam?

There is no fixed official split, but past papers consistently show 8–12 basic mathematics MCQs in the General Ability portion of the 100-question paper.

Can I skip math and still pass PPSC?

You can pass, but you're throwing away the easiest marks on the paper. Most aspirants skip math, which means mastering it gives you a relative advantage over the competition — 10 marks often equals the final merit gap.

Which math topics are most important for PPSC?

Percentages first, then ratio and proportion, averages, basic algebra identities, profit/loss/interest, and speed–time–distance with age problems. Together these cover nearly every math MCQ asked.
